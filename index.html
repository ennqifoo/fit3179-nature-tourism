Australia's Nature Tourism

Is nature tourism back?

86.4Mregional trips in 2024
29 of 72regions above 2019 levels
+24%NT spend per trip, 2026

1How big is it, and what happened?

COVID-19 lockdowns and border closures cut trips in every state in 2020–21. By 2024, only Queensland was back above its 2019 level.

Trips against 2019, by state

  • Every state fell sharply in 2020–21.
  • Only Queensland is back above 2019, at 101.
  • The Northern Territory is furthest behind, at 84.

Latest year: who grew, who fell

  • Queensland grew most: +1.4%, or 368,000 more trips.
  • The Northern Territory fell most: −1.5%.
  • Every state moved by less than 2%.

Where visitors stay

  • Rented homes rose from 42% to 46% of nights.
  • Friends' homes fell from 33% to 29%.
  • Hotels and motels stayed near 11%.

2Where does it happen?

Nature tourism is spread unevenly. These four maps show recovery, regional trips, international gateways and state size.

Recovery by tourism region

  • Desert and outback regions are furthest behind 2019.
  • Kakadu is at 59% and Alice Springs at 67%.
  • 29 of 72 regions are above 2019.

Regional trips, 2024

  • Each dot is 100,000 trips.
  • NSW, Victoria and Queensland hold four in five dots.
  • The NT has only 7 dots, and two overlap.

International gateways

  • Sydney and Melbourne take the most international trips.
  • Sydney has 3.8 million trips and Melbourne 2.7 million.
  • The Gold Coast and Tropical North Queensland lead the regions.

States resized by trips

  • Circle area shows trips, not land area.
  • NSW is the biggest, at 39.2 million trips.
  • Tiny Tasmania and the ACT stay visible.

3Who is winning the recovery?

Some regions have bounced back past 2019 while others keep falling. Visitors to remote places also spend more on each trip.

Biggest risers and fallers

  • Six regions grew and six fell between 2019 and 2024.
  • Tasmania's North West grew most, by 19.5%.
  • The Flinders Ranges fell most, by 25%.

Regional rebound leaders

  • Tasmania's North West leads, up 19.5%.
  • Victoria's Lakes follows, up 17.3%.
  • Victoria has five of the top 15 regions.

Spend per trip

  • The NT spends the most per trip: $1,811.
  • That is up 24% on a year ago.
  • Queensland fell 6% and the ACT fell 13%.

Ranking of states

  • Queensland climbed from 4th to 1st.
  • Tasmania is second, just behind Queensland.
  • The Northern Territory slid from 6th to last.

4When, how and why do people travel?

Seasons shape when people visit, from Tasmania's summer to the Top End's dry season. Holidays are the main reason to travel.

Seasons

  • The NT peaks in the dry season: 130 in September.
  • Its wet-season low is 67 in March.
  • Tasmania peaks in summer, at 119 in March.

Why people travel

  • Holidays make up 46% of trips.
  • Visiting friends and relatives is 38%.
  • Business is only 8%.

What visitors spend on

  • Accommodation is the biggest item, at 28% of spend.
  • Flights and eating out add 15% each.
  • Together these three make up 58% of spend.

What it means

  • The recovery is uneven. Only Queensland is back above 2019 (101). The NT sits at 84, and 29 of 72 regions are above 2019.
  • Nature tourism is concentrated. NSW, Victoria and Queensland take four in five regional trips. Tasmania's North West (+19.5%) leads the rebound.
  • Remote places earn more per visitor. The NT's spend per trip ($1,811, up 24%) is the highest. Its visitors swing between 130 in the dry season and 67 in the wet.
  • Why people come. Holidays are 46% of trips. Accommodation, flights and eating out make up 58% of spending.
