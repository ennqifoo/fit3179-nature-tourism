Australia's Nature Tourism

Is nature tourism back?

86.4 millionregional trips in 2024
29 of 72regions above 2019 levels
+24%rise in Northern Territory spend per trip, 2026

1Every state fell in 2020–21, but only Queensland has fully recovered

  • COVID-19 lockdowns cut trips in every state.
  • Border closures hit hardest in 2020–21.
  • By 2024, only Queensland beat its 2019 level.

Trips against 2019, by state

  • Every state fell together, so lockdowns were the cause.
  • Queensland is the only state above 2019 (index 101).
  • At 84, the Northern Territory is still 16% below 2019.

Latest year: who grew, who fell

  • Queensland added 368,000 trips; the Northern Territory lost 15,000.
  • A small rise in a big state means many trips.
  • All states moved by less than 2%: steady, not a boom.

Ranking of states

  • Queensland rose from 4th to 1st: the recovery leader.
  • Rank hides size: the Australian Capital Territory was 1st in 2023 but is tiny.
  • The Northern Territory fell from 6th to last: slowest to recover.

2Trips are concentrated in the east, and remote regions are slowest to recover

  • So where is it happening? Four maps show where.
  • They cover recovery, regional trips, gateways and state size.
  • Most trips sit on the east coast.

Recovery by tourism region

  • Remote desert regions are slowest to recover.
  • Kakadu has lost 41% of its trips; Alice Springs a third.
  • Only 29 of 72 regions beat 2019, so recovery is patchy.

Regional trips, 2024

  • Four in five trips go to three eastern states.
  • So the nature market is concentrated on the east coast.
  • The Northern Territory has few dots but high spend per trip.

International gateways

  • Sydney and Melbourne are the main arrival gates.
  • Sydney’s 3.8 million is 1.4 times Melbourne’s 2.7 million.
  • The Gold Coast and Tropical North Queensland are the nature gateways.

States resized by trips

  • New South Wales is biggest (39.2 million) but grew only 0.5%.
  • Queensland is only third in size, yet grew fastest.
  • The Northern Territory is tiny, so small changes look large.

3Tasmania and Victoria lead the rebound, but the Northern Territory earns the most per trip

  • Next, who is winning the recovery?
  • Some regions beat 2019 while others keep falling.
  • Remote trips earn more: mostly transport, accommodation and food.

Biggest risers and fallers

  • Six regions beat 2019 while six fell: place matters.
  • Tasmania’s North West gained nearly one in five trips.
  • The Flinders Ranges lost a quarter of its trips.

Regional rebound leaders

  • Victoria has five of the top 15 regions, so its rebound is broad.
  • Queensland has four, all in the middle of the ranking.
  • Tasmania’s North West leads at 19.5%, then Victoria’s Lakes at 17.3%.

Spend per trip

  • The Northern Territory earns the most per trip: $1,811, up 24%.
  • Remote trips are longer, so each visitor spends more.
  • Queensland fell 6%: more trips, but less spend on each.

What visitors spend on

  • Transport is the biggest group at 30%, just ahead of accommodation (28%).
  • Food and drink takes 25%, mostly restaurants and takeaways.
  • These three groups take 83%; the rest is spread thinly.

4People come for holidays, stay in rented homes and follow the seasons

  • Finally, how, when and why do people travel?
  • Seasons shape visits: Tasmania peaks in summer, the Top End in the dry season.
  • Holidays are the main reason to travel.

Seasons

  • Top End visitors come in the dry season: 130 in September.
  • The wet season empties it: 67 in March.
  • Tasmania is the mirror: a summer peak of 119 in March.

Where visitors stay

  • Rented homes rose while friends’ homes fell.
  • So visitors are shifting from free stays to paid ones.
  • Hotels stayed near 11%, so the shift is not hotel-led.

Why people travel

  • Holidays and visiting friends make up 84% of trips.
  • Friends and family are nearly as big as holidays.
  • Business is only 8%: nature tourism is mostly leisure.

What it means

  • The recovery is uneven. Only Queensland is back above 2019 (101). The Northern Territory sits at 84, and 29 of 72 regions are above 2019.
  • Nature tourism is concentrated. New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland take four in five regional trips. Tasmania's North West (+19.5%) leads the rebound.
  • Remote places earn more per visitor. The Northern Territory's spend per trip ($1,811, up 24%) is the highest. Its visitors swing between 130 in the dry season and 67 in the wet.
  • Why people come. Holidays are 46% of trips. Transport, accommodation, and food and drink make up 83% of spending.

Data sources

  1. Tourism Research Australia (TRA): National Visitor Survey and Domestic Tourism Statistics (year ending December 2024; June quarter 2026) and the International Visitor Survey (June quarter 2026).
  2. Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS): Australian Statistical Geography Standard state boundaries (CC BY 4.0), with Tourism Research Australia region boundaries (2026).

Notes. Trip indices compare each year with 2019 (=100). The National Visitor Survey changed method in 2014, so earlier years are not directly comparable. Spend per trip, state ranks and quarterly seasonality indices are my own calculations from the published tables. Dots on the dot map are placed randomly inside each region; the cartogram and treemap layouts are computed, not published. Some tourism regions were renamed in 2026 and matched to the survey names by hand. Figures marked "np" by TRA are not published and are left blank.
